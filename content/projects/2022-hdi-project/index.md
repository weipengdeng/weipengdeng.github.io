## Overview

Many rapidly urbanizing regions are shifting from outward expansion to quality-oriented renewal within existing built-up areas. In practice, the most challenging spaces are often those with high building height, high density, and high development intensity—areas that can deliver strong economic and social benefits, yet may also accumulate risks related to comfort, safety, environmental stress, and uneven performance. This project develops a scalable, data-driven technical pathway to identify, diagnose, and improve such high-intensity built environments across diverse city contexts, while keeping the workflow efficient, explainable, and suitable for planning and design decision support.

## Why this projectg
Conventional planning controls typically rely on a small set of indicators (e.g., height, floor area ratio, coverage). However, modern cities are shaped by multi-dimensional 3D form, street networks, open space systems, land-use mixtures, and human activity patterns. As a result, “one-size-fits-all” thresholds may be too coarse, and traditional simulation-heavy evaluation can be too slow or costly for iterative urban renewal. This project addresses the gap by combining:

  *Multi-source urban data integration (built form, networks, land cover, activity proxies, remote sensing)

  *Multi-scale spatial analytics to capture stable patterns across resolutions

  *Explainable machine learning to extract a minimal, transferable indicator set

  *Multi-objective performance reasoning to locate low-performing yet high-potential areas

  *Generative AI (image-to-image translation) to enable rapid scenario testing and design feedback

## Core objectives
  *Standardize the identification of high-intensity built-up types across cities and spatial scales, with an interpretable typology.

  *Reduce complexity by learning a minimal indicator subset that preserves classification power and improves replicability.

  *Build a performance “atlas” that jointly reflects environmental, social, and economic dimensions at fine spatial granularity.

  *Prioritize intervention areas using a multi-objective framework (i.e., recognizing trade-offs rather than a single score).

  *Provide practical design guidance by learning non-linear “reasonable ranges” and aspirational “target ranges” for key controls.

  *Enable rapid design iteration through a lightweight generative simulation engine that approximates performance outcomes without heavy, time-consuming physics-based modeling.
